Introduction

Depression and related mental health disorders are major global health issues that affect people across age groups and social backgrounds. Conventional diagnostic pathways mostly depend on clinical interviews and self-reported assessments, which are often subjective, time-consuming, and difficult to scale for large populations.

With rising social media usage, users frequently express emotional states, isolation, hopelessness, and distress in textual form. Platforms such as Reddit contain large volumes of posts discussing anxiety, depression, and suicidal ideation. This creates an opportunity for automated early-risk detection using natural language processing.

Recent advances in AI allow us to transform raw text into contextual vectors and classify risk patterns with higher consistency. The proposed work combines MiniLM embeddings, classical machine learning, and LLM-based zero/few-shot prompting in a unified comparative framework for scalable, assistive mental health screening.

Unlike isolated model studies, this project emphasizes reproducible end-to-end experimentation, cross-domain generalization checks, and error analysis to better understand strengths, weaknesses, and practical deployment boundaries.

Problem Definition & Objectives

The major challenge is to accurately identify mental health risk from unstructured social media text where language is noisy, informal, and context dependent. Distress can be expressed implicitly through metaphors, fragmented writing, or indirect cues, making simple keyword and sentiment systems unreliable.

Another core challenge is building a complete and consistent pipeline from preprocessing to evaluation. Classical models may face transfer issues across datasets, while LLM methods can be unstable due to prompt sensitivity and interpretability limitations.

Primary Objectives:

  • Design an end-to-end framework for automated depression-risk detection from Reddit text
  • Develop robust text preprocessing that removes noise while preserving risk-relevant cues
  • Use MiniLM sentence embeddings for context-rich semantic representation
  • Train and compare Logistic Regression, SVM, and MLP as strong supervised baselines
  • Evaluate prompt-based zero-shot and few-shot LLM methods under consistent conditions
  • Measure performance using accuracy, precision, recall, weighted F1, and confusion matrices
  • Analyze cross-domain transfer (Reddit ↔ DAIC) and detailed error categories
  • Support ethical assistive screening with human-in-the-loop interpretation
Utility, Ethics & Social Relevance

The framework supports early assistive screening by helping moderators, counsellors, and wellness teams prioritize posts that may require urgent review. It does not replace diagnosis; instead, it acts as a triage signal to improve response timeliness.

From a practical standpoint, MiniLM plus classical models offers efficient CPU-based execution suitable for academic labs and resource-constrained environments. This lowers infrastructure cost while preserving meaningful performance.

Ethically, the system emphasizes cautious interpretation, high-risk recall awareness, and expert oversight before any intervention. It can contribute to scalable digital mental health monitoring while balancing automation with responsibility.

Preprocessing: URL removal, special-character filtering, normalization, duplicate/empty-post handling, and quality control while preserving psychologically meaningful cues.

Feature Extraction: all-MiniLM-L6-v2 converts each post into dense contextual vectors that better capture semantic similarity than surface-level word counts.

Classical Modelling: Logistic Regression, SVM, and MLP are trained on shared embeddings to maintain consistency across model comparisons.

LLM Evaluation: zero-shot and few-shot prompts are applied to compare training-free inference with supervised baselines and study prompt sensitivity.

Evaluation: accuracy, precision, recall, weighted F1, confusion matrices, and cross-domain transfer metrics are used for robust analysis.

System Design Considerations
  • Reliability: stratified cross-validation and transfer experiments to verify model stability
  • Interpretability: structured error categories and comparison of ML vs LLM decision behavior
  • Efficiency: MiniLM embedding reuse and lightweight classifiers for low-cost execution
  • Scalability: modular architecture supporting new datasets, prompts, and model modules
  • Safety: high-risk predictions treated as assistive alerts with human review requirements
  • Ethics: emphasis on responsible use, non-diagnostic output framing, and caution on bias
  • Reproducibility: stage-wise notebook documentation of preprocessing, modeling, and metrics

Results & Analysis

The system was evaluated across model performance, cross-domain transfer, and prompt sensitivity. Results show that embedding-based classical models achieved stronger and more stable outcomes than training-free prompting alone.

SVM achieved the best overall supervised performance (~79% accuracy, ~0.79 weighted F1), while Logistic Regression provided a consistent baseline and MLP offered competitive non-linear modeling behavior.

LLM zero-shot/few-shot methods demonstrated useful low-data potential but remained prompt-sensitive and less stable, especially in high-risk edge cases where false negatives are critical.

Conclusion

This project successfully delivers a unified and reproducible framework for text-based depression risk detection by combining MiniLM embeddings, classical supervised models, and LLM prompting strategies within one comparative system.

Findings show that supervised embedding-based models currently provide stronger consistency and accuracy, while LLMs offer flexible low-data inference but require careful prompt design and safety-aware interpretation.
